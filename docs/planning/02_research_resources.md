# Research & Resources

These are sources gathered in Oct 2026 for the game. Facts are labeled **[sourced]** (found in the linked source), **[recall]** (from general knowledge, not yet checked), or **[conflict]** (sources disagree). Before writing any event text, check its facts against at least one source here.

---

## 1. The Great Trek: overview

- **Dates:** c. 1835 to the early or mid-1840s. **[sourced]**
- **Numbers:** about 12,000–14,000 Boers. One source estimates a roughly equal number of Khoikhoi and Black servants travelled with them. **[sourced/conflict]**
- **Two main directions:** north across the Orange River onto the Highveld, or north-east towards Natal. **[sourced]**
- **Main parties:**
  - **Van Rensburg** (49 people): headed north and was killed by an impi of Soshangane. **[sourced]**
  - **Louis Tregardt (Trichardt):** Soutpansberg, then to Delagoa Bay, arriving 13 Apr 1838. About 27 of 53 died, mostly of malaria, including Tregardt and his wife Martha. **[sourced]**
  - **Hendrik Potgieter:** attacked by the Ndebele (Mzilikazi). **Battle of Vegkop**, Oct 1836 (16th or 20th) **[conflict]**. Afterwards Moroka of the Barolong sent oxen to bring the stranded trek to **Thaba Nchu**. **[sourced]**
  - **Gert Maritz, Piet Retief, Piet Uys, Sarel Cilliers.** Retief's manifesto was published in the *Graham's Town Journal* in Jan 1837. **[sourced]**
- **Natal sequence:**
  - Retief camped at **Kerkenberg** with 54 wagons (5 Oct 1837) and descended the Drakensberg by **Retief's Pass**. **[sourced]**
  - Retief and his party were killed at Dingane's capital uMgungundlovu (Feb 1838), followed by attacks on the camps (Bloukrans/Weenen) and the Battle of **Blood River / Ncome** (16 Dec 1838). **[sourced/recall]**
  - Then came the Republic of Natalia, the British siege of Port Natal, and the Battle of Congella (1842). **[sourced]**
- **Reasons given for the trek:**
  - Retief's manifesto lists labour and "vagrancy" grievances, frontier insecurity, and British rule.
  - Emancipation of the enslaved (effective 1834–38) and the compensation complaints. How much these mattered is debated among historians. **[sourced]**

## 2. Primary sources (diaries and journals)

- **Louis Tregardt's diary (1836–38).** He was the only trek leader who kept a diary. It is the best source for daily life, illness, oxen and trade.
  - English edition, translated by Jackie Grobler: https://litera.co.za/english/index.php/book/louis-tregardt-se-dagboek-1836-1838/
  - UP article on the diary as a source: https://repository.up.ac.za/items/f9f55a7f-f2bf-4e72-b961-25c05d806722
- **Anna Steenkamp's journal** (Retief's niece). A family chronicle of the Trek, often quoted on why families left. Studied in A.W.G. Raath (NWU, 2016/17): https://repository.nwu.ac.za/handle/10394/20325
- **Susanna Smit's spiritual diary.** She was the wife of predikant Erasmus Smit; the diary is over 1000 pages, held in the Natal Archives, and transcribed in M. Puddu, Wits MA 1996: https://wiredspace.wits.ac.za/items/950155f9-10e4-48a1-9e53-d9fa0994e9c5. This is an excellent source for a **Predikant family** class.
- **Sarel Cilliers.** Preacher, and the man behind the Blood River vow: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarel_Cilliers
- **Graham's Town Journal** (1830s). The newspaper that printed Retief's manifesto. Worth looking for digitised copies.

## 3. Material culture: wagon, guns, food, money

- **Kakebeenwa** ("jawbone wagon", named for its shape). Oxen were yoked in pairs with a wooden **juk** (yoke) held by a **jukskei** (yoke pin). **[sourced]** Ox wagon: https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/Ox_wagon
- **Speed:** with herds alongside, about **10 km/day** is typical; oxen walked about 3 mph on rough roads, with a long midday halt. **[sourced]** Use about 6–12 miles/day as the base pace.
- **Oldest wagons:** the Msunduzi Museum holds the second-oldest ox-wagon in SA (1824). **[sourced]**
- **Guns:** *sanna* (the Voortrekker flintlock), *roer* (a heavy hunting gun), *snaphaan*, *sterloop*. **[sourced]** Dictionary of South African English: https://dsae.co.za/entry/sanna/e06233 · https://dsae.co.za/entry/roer/e06042 · https://dsae.co.za/entry/snaphaan/e06656
- **Food:** beskuit (rusks), biltong, dried peaches, coffee, sugar. Women managed provisions. Wagons carried medicine chests and beds, and chicken coops hung underneath. **[sourced, secondary]**
  - https://www.south-african-homeschool-curriculum.com/the-great-trek
  - https://weet.co.za/skoolwerk/wie-was-die-voortrekkers/
- **Money:** from 1825, 1 rixdollar = 1s 6d, so **£1 ≈ 13⅓ Rds**. **[sourced]**
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rixdollar
  - https://dsae.co.za/entry/rixdollar/e06026
- **Dictionary of South African English (dsae.co.za)** is the best tool for checking period words (smous, laager, voorloper, trekboer, drift, spoor, veld...).

## 4. Places on a likely game route

Graaff-Reinet / Grahamstown → Colesberg area → **Orange River drifts** → Philippolis (Griqua) → **Thaba Nchu** (Barolong, Moroka; Wesleyan mission) → Vegkop / Sand River → **Kerkenberg** → Drakensberg descent (Retief's Pass) → Tugela / Bloukrans → Port Natal / Pietermaritzburg.

Rough total distance: about 950 km (≈600 mi). This is my own estimate and should be checked against a map. Retief's party left the Grahamstown area in early 1837 and reached Kerkenberg in Oct 1837, so the journey took many months, not weeks.

Optional northern branch (a "hard mode" route): Soutpansberg → Delagoa Bay (Tregardt). Malaria and tsetse dominate this route.

## 5. African polities and perspectives

You need these to write the people the trekkers met as full participants rather than obstacles:

- **Barolong** under Moroka at Thaba Nchu (allies after Vegkop)
- **Griqua** at Philippolis
- **Basotho** under Moshoeshoe
- **Ndebele** under Mzilikazi
- **Zulu kingdom** under Dingane, later Mpande
- **Gaza / Soshangane**
- **Xhosa** on the Eastern Cape frontier (for the English storyline)

- **The Mfecane debate.** The idea that the interior was "empty" because of Zulu-driven wars was used to justify settler land claims. Julian Cobbing (1988) challenged it, and it has been debated since.
  - UCT Emandulo: https://studio-emandulo.uct.ac.za/the-mfecane-debate-landing-page-copy/
  - SAHO: Mfecane: https://www.sahistory.org.za/article/mfecane
  - *The Mfecane Aftermath* (Wits UP, 1995): https://archivewitspress.co.za/catalogue/the-mfecane-aftermath
- **Port Natal English traders** (Biggar family, Dick King) took part in 1838–42 events. They are a natural link for **English-speaking characters inside the Trek story**.
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biggar_family
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dick_King
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Congella

## 6. English settlers and the Trek

- **The 1820 Settlers' Bible for the Uys trek (April 1837).** The British settlers of Grahamstown, led by Thomas Philipps and W.R. Thompson, gave a large Dutch Bible to the departing Uys party. It is now displayed at the Voortrekker Monument. **This is a ready-made scene of English and Dutch speakers meeting.** **[sourced]**

## 6b. Sources for the northern route and the English side (added after decisions)

- **W. Cornwallis Harris, 1836–37.**
  - He outfitted at Grahamstown, crossed the Orange to Kuruman (Moffat), and visited Mzilikazi; he was at the king's headquarters when news came that trekkers were crossing the Vaal.
  - On his return he met the Ndebele force coming back from Vegkop. He was back at the Cape in Jan 1837.
  - Book: *Narrative of an Expedition from the Cape of Good Hope… 1836–37* (Bombay 1838), later published as *The Wild Sports of Southern Africa*. *Portraits of the Game and Wild Animals of Southern Africa* (1840) is public-domain art for hunting screens.
  - This is the best English-perspective primary source for the northern route in the same years. **[sourced]**
  - Sources:
    - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Cornwallis_Harris
    - https://news.uct.ac.za/article/-2002-11-19-rare-book-for-african-studies-library
    - SAHO: Mzilikazi: https://sahistory.org.za/people/king-mzilikazi
- **Sixth Frontier War (Dec 1834–1836).**
  - It started over the Kat River lands.
  - King Hintsa was killed in 1835 while held under a truce.
  - Queen Adelaide Province was annexed in May 1835 and the annexation was reversed by Glenelg (Dec 1835–Dec 1836). Many frontier farmers saw that reversal as a betrayal.
  - Background for both sides' backstories. **[sourced]**
  - Sources:
    - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hintsa_kaKhawuta
    - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Kaffraria
    - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1835_in_South_Africa
- **Potchefstroom.**
  - Founded by Potgieter on the Mooi River in Dec 1838 or June 1839 **[conflict]**.
  - It formed the Winburg–Potchefstroom republic until 1840.
  - Barolong and Bahurutshe communities lived in the area before the trekkers. **[sourced]**
  - Sources:
    - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potchefstroom
    - https://www.theheritageportal.co.za/article/founding-potchefstroom
- **Mosega (Jan 1837) and the Marico campaign (Nov 1837)**, where trekker commandos drove the Ndebele north, and the American (ABCFM) mission at Mosega. **[recall, verify]**
- **English speakers on the Trek** were rare. I found no good source listing them, which is a research gap. Andrew Smith (English) scouted Natal in 1832. Port Natal traders took part in the 1838 events.
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Trek

## 8. Game-design references

- **The Oregon Trail (MECC, 1985).** R. Philip Bouchard led the design. His book *You Have Died of Dysentery: The Creation of Oregon Trail* explains how pace, rations, river crossings, hunting and the tombstone mechanics were chosen. **Read this first.**
  - https://thevideogamelibrary.org/book/you-have-died-of-dysentery-the-creation-of-oregon-trail
  - https://news.uga.edu/creating-80s-oregon-trail/
- **When Rivers Were Trails** (Elizabeth LaPensée, Indian Land Tenure Foundation, 2019). An Oregon-Trail-style game told from an Anishinaabe perspective, written by 20–30+ Indigenous writers; it won the IndieCade 2019 Adaptation Award. This is the model for showing the people a trek moves through as full characters.
  - https://indianlandtenure.itch.io/when-rivers-were-trails
  - https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2020/08/13/from-pixelated-to-post-colonial-iconic-1980s-oregon-trail-video-game-gets-an-indigenous-reboot
- **Epic Games: "Representing history: the many faces of The Oregon Trail"**: https://store.epicgames.com/news/representing-history-the-many-faces-of-the-oregon-trail
- **ink / inkjs** (inkle's narrative scripting, exportable to the web). It has no official localisation workflow; the community approach is tagging lines with IDs. Useful if the writing gets very branchy, but harder for Dutch/English.
  - https://github.com/inkle/inky
  - https://johnnemann.medium.com/localizing-ink-with-unity-42a4cf3590f3
